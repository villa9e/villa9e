## Can investors buy ViCo?

**Before exchange listing (launch → ~Month 10–12):** $VICO is earn-only. No one can buy
it. Intentional — the founding distribution goes entirely to people who did real work, not
speculators. Every early holder earned it.

**First liquidity (at $10K MRR — ViCo activation):** contracts deploy, conversions open, a
DEX liquidity pool (Uniswap on Polygon / QuickSwap) pairs $VICO with MATIC or USDC.
Technically anyone can swap, but it needs crypto knowledge. **Liquidity locked 1 year** as
a public, on-chain-verifiable trust signal (Village cannot rug it).

**Centralized listings (Year 2):** Tier 3 (MEXC, Gate.io, Bitget) first — need clean audit,
legal opinion on token classification, demonstrated DEX volume, listing fee. Then Tier 2
(KuCoin, Bybit, OKX). Tier 1 (Coinbase, Binance) is a Year 3–5 goal needing massive
sustained volume + regulatory clarity.

**Early investors:** can negotiate a **pre-listing arrangement** — $VICO at a negotiated
rate from the **strategic reserve** (10% of 33M = 3.3M set aside for partnerships). Private,
qualified-investor, appropriate legal structure, lock-up. Same structure most major crypto
projects use pre-listing. Once listed, the position is liquid.

## Is it disruptive technology? Yes (Christensen sense)

Disrupts and the incumbents structurally cannot respond:
- **TikTok/Instagram** — paying creators 85% of the value their content generates collapses
  their business model.
- **LinkedIn** — built on self-reported credentials; can't add blockchain verification to
  data it doesn't control the truth of. Village creates the verified-achievement standard.
- **Coursera/Udemy/Masterclass** — sell education as a product; Village makes education a
  step in a verified goal-execution system that earns crypto. They can't add GPS + ViCo
  without becoming Village.
- **Data brokers (Acxiom, Oracle Data Cloud)** — make money taking data without paying
  users; Village pays users 70%. No mechanism to pay without eliminating their margin.
- **Neobanks (Chime, Cash App)** — compete on features; Village is a life operating system
  (bank tied to goals, ViCo wallet, eStore revenue, Data Locker earnings).

Village doesn't beat them at their game — it changes the game; incumbents are too invested
in the current game to switch.

## Is it a moonshot? Yes

A moonshot changes something fundamental about how civilization operates (printing press,
internet, Bitcoin). Village's thesis: it changes the **relationship between human effort and
financial compensation.** Since the industrial revolution, the mechanism has been
employment — the org captures the surplus of your labor. The creator economy tried to change
it and failed (platforms still capture most value). Village introduces **verified human
progress as a mining operation for a fixed-supply asset**: every person who improves their
life generates value that compounds in their wallet, not a platform's revenue report.

Scale if it works: ~5B internet users; even 500M serious users makes early Phase-1 $VICO
extraordinarily valuable as they convert at Phase 2/3/4 rates. This is the Bitcoin moonshot
thesis applied to human development — the underlying asset is human growth, not computation.
Second-order: Village-native income replacing traditional employment, a large middle class
of creators, users sharing in their own data's value, higher goal-completion rates with
downstream effects on education/health/mobility.

**Honest assessment:** most moonshots fail. Village's depends on several things going right
simultaneously (real $VICO market value, meaningfully better completion rates, enough
creators, surviving to network-effect scale). It has the *architecture* of a moonshot —
mechanism, timing, market conditions are right. Even conservative/realistic scenarios are a
significant, profitable business; the moonshot is the upside.
